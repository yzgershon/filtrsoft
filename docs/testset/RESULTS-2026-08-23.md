## The worst traps found

### Xbox Series X 2TB Galaxy Black — $1,850, Lynn — **ABSURD**
Claims brand new, factory sealed, with receipt. That edition retailed around **$600–730**. Asking price is **~2.5–3× retail**.
Posted **March 2**, still live in late August. **Six months unsold is the market's verdict.**

**Methodology note worth keeping:** when a summarizer was asked why it was $1,850, it *invented* a justification ("limited editions command higher prices," "market scarcity"). That is precisely the failure mode Filtrsoft must not have. **The engine anchors on sold comps, never on the seller's framing or on plausible-sounding narrative.**

### NVIDIA Tesla P100 16GB — $80, Northborough — **NOT A GPU**
Seller describes it as *"designed for professionals in animation, video editing."*
Photo confirms the I/O bracket is a **solid vented grille with zero display connectors.** No HDMI, no DisplayPort. It is also **passively cooled**, built for server chassis airflow, and will overheat in a desktop.
$80 is roughly fair for a CUDA compute card. It is worthless for the use the seller advertises.

### 2019 5K iMac A2115 — $295, Downtown Boston — **PASS**
Described as *"like NEW"* while the same description discloses a **cracked bottom panel and an LCD that is detached with incomplete installation, held with tape.**
On a 5K iMac the panel is glued; a partially reinstalled screen risks dust ingress, cable damage, and flex.
**Detectable pattern: internal contradiction between the condition claim and the disclosed defects.** 4+ months unsold at a "firm, non-negotiable" price.

### Acer Aspire E15 — $275, Needham — **~2× value.** Budget 2017 laptop, worth $100–150.
### Apple iMac A1311 "needs OS installed" — $165, Brookline — 2009–2011 machine worth $60–100. "Needs OS" reads as untested or locked.
### Meta Quest 3S — $300, Lynn — at or **above** retail for a "sealed" unit.

## The scam case study

### iPhone 15 Pro Max — $500, Brockton — **DO NOT PURSUE**

Both photos are **screenshots of the iOS Messages photo viewer**, taken at 8:08 on a phone at 89% battery. The tell is the bottom toolbar: it carries a **reply arrow and a save icon**. You only get a save icon on a photo *someone sent you*. **The seller is screenshotting pictures another person sent them.**

The lock screen reads **"Tuesday, August 26."** August 26 fell on a Tuesday in **2025**. The photo is roughly a year old.

Stacked signals:
- Photos are screenshots of images received from someone else
- Photo predates the listing by ~9 months
- Posted **May 21**, still live in August — 3 months unsold, far under market
- **No storage size** (a $200+ swing on this model), no battery health, no IMEI
- 14-word description
- One of **five** near-identical "Unlocked iPhone" listings from Brockton

### The refinement this produced

**Listing age is a signal, not a verdict — it must be weighted by listing quality.**

Both the Brockton iPhone and the Dell Latitude sat ~3 months. One is a scam; the other is the best deal on the board. The difference is that the Dell has 11 photos, a detailed spec list, and *voluntarily disclosed defects*. Long time-on-market means "underpriced for a hidden reason" only when the listing is also low-information.

## The most important result — and it's a miss, not a win

### Gaming PC "SPECS IN PICTURES" — $750, Dedham — **BUY**

**CORRECTED 2026-08-23 after Yish's audit. My first-pass verdict was wrong.**

The listing has three photos. I read A and B, **skipped C to save tokens**, and returned INSUFFICIENT_DATA on the grounds that the photos showed only brand names — Gigabyte board, Gigabyte GeForce RTX card, NZXT Kraken AIO, T-Force RAM.

Yish opened photo C. It is a slightly blurry shot of the Windows **System → About** screen containing the entire spec sheet:

| | |
|---|---|
| CPU | **AMD Ryzen 5 9600X** 6-core (Zen 5) |
| GPU | **NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 (12 GB)** |
| RAM | 16 GB |
| Storage | 582 GB of 932 GB used |
| OS | Windows 11 Home, installed **7/8/2026** |

Revalued:

| Component | Used value |
|---|---|
| RTX 5070 12GB | $450–550 |
| Ryzen 5 9600X | $180–220 |
| Gigabyte AM5 board | $90–130 |
| 16GB DDR5 | $45–60 |
| ~1TB NVMe | $50–70 |
| NZXT Kraken AIO w/ LCD | $110–160 |
| NZXT case + PSU | $100–160 |
| **Sum of parts** | **$1,025–1,350** |

At **$750** the GPU alone accounts for $450–550, meaning the Zen 5 CPU, AM5 board, DDR5, LCD AIO, case, PSU and 1TB cost roughly **$200–300**. Part-out realizes $850–1,100. **This is a genuine deal.**

Open question: Windows was installed **7/8/2026**, so the build is six weeks old. Current-gen parts moving that fast warrants a "why are you selling?" PSU is still unknown, which matters on a 5070.

### The three rules this produced

1. **Read every photo. Never sample.** The funnel may drop *listings* at any stage, but once a listing reaches the vision stage it must never drop *photos*. Token savings that skip an image are false economy — the skipped one is exactly where the seller put the specs.
2. **Spec screens are the highest-value photo type.** Windows System→About, Task Manager, HWiNFO, iOS Settings→About. Detect them early (mostly-screen, text-dense) and process at maximum available resolution. Sellers photograph these constantly.
3. **"Insufficient data" must be earned by reading everything.** The verdict category is correct and still essential — but reaching it by sampling is a bug wearing the costume of caution. A confident "I don't know" that's actually laziness is as damaging as a confident wrong number.

**The framework held. The execution didn't.** Worth recording that the miss was caught by a human doing what the eval loop exists to do.

## Findings that change the product

1. **The strongest fraud signals are not in the item photos.** They are in listing age, missing spec fields, cluster patterns across a seller's postings, and **screenshot artifacts in the image chrome** (iOS toolbars, photo counters, close buttons). None of this requires vision, all of it is cheap, and no competitor computes it.
2. **Listing age must be weighted by listing quality.** Long time-on-market plus low information equals hidden problem. Long time-on-market plus a detailed honest listing equals cosmetic damage scaring off consumers, which is a flipper's opportunity.
3. **Internal contradictions are detectable and high-value.** "Like NEW" beside a disclosed detached LCD. "Gen 4 NVMe" on a platform incapable of PCIe Gen 4.
4. **Sellers photograph spec screens constantly** — Windows System>About, HWiNFO, Task Manager. Reading them is high value and resolution-bound. Craigslist caps at 1200×900; that was too small to read one of them.
5. **"Insufficient data" has to be a first-class verdict**, not a fallback. Roughly 1 in 10 listings genuinely cannot be valued from what's posted.
6. **Cluster detection across a single seller or a single town is a strong prior.** Five near-identical Brockton iPhone listings.
