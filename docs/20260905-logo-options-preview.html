Filtrsoft — nine logo options

DECIDED 2026-09-05 — 01 The Cut, and it is shipped.
Yish: "i want 01 filter and a gradient dot on the i uncapitalized f filtrsoft". So the mark is 01 and the wordmark is row three of the treatments below: all lowercase, all ink, brand gradient on the tittle of the i and nowhere else.

Built in web/src/brand.tsx (one component, replacing the wordmark that was copy-pasted into five places), .wmi in 02-shell.css, web/public/icon.svg and src/browser/make-icons.mjs. The rest of this page is the record of what was rejected and why — it is history now, not a menu. Do not re-open it without a reason.

This is the specimen sheet the decision was made from: nine marks drawn in the app's real palette, shown as an app icon, as a lockup, at 16 px, in one flat colour, and on a white background.

Why this sheet is not 1440×900. Every other preview I build is a fixed window at 1:1, because proportion is the thing being judged. A logo is judged at its real sizes instead, so the sizes below are literal: the 16 px row really is 16 px, the icons really are 88. The one place a window matters, the app's top bar, is drawn at the real 360 px phone width further down.

06 and 03 fail here. The sieve's nine cells collapse into a grey square with a smudge, and the chevrons' 2.4 px strokes go to under one device pixel. 02, 04 and 08 are still themselves; 08 is far and away the strongest. That is most of the decision.

And 01 has a real problem at 16 px, which I only saw after rendering it. Its top bar is drawn at 40% opacity and its middle bar at 75%, which is the contrast that makes the narrowing readable at 88 px. At 16 those two bars nearly disappear and it reads as one gradient dot with dust above it. The fix is not to abandon the mark, it is to ship a separate favicon variant at 70/90/100% — logos routinely have a small-size cut, and make-icons.mjs already writes four files from one source so the hook exists. But it is extra work that 08 does not need, and you should know that before picking.

The wordmark, four ways

Independent of which mark wins. What ships today is the first row.

today — split filtrsoft
gradient on the f only filtrsoft
gradient dot on the i fltrsoft
all ink, mark carries it filtrsoft
capitalised Filtrsoft

Rows two and three put the brand colour in one small place instead of across half the word, which starts to matter once a mark is also carrying the gradient. Row two ties the wordmark to option 08. Row three swaps the i for a dotless i and draws the tittle as a gradient square, so at 19 px the colour is a single 3 px dot. Row four is the one to use if you pick a gradient mark, because otherwise the gradient appears twice in one lockup and the identity gets diluted, which is exactly what 02-shell.css already warns against.

What made me pick 01

  • It is the only one that survives all four stress tests. 16 px, one colour, on white, and beside a coloured verdict chip. 03 and 06 fail small, 04 fails in mono, 08 fails next to the chips.
  • The gradient lands on exactly one element. 02-shell.css says the gradient's repetition is the identity and spreading it dilutes it. 01 uses it once, on the shape that means "the deal".
  • It draws the actual number. 4,482 to 28. Wide, narrower, one.
  • Three rectangles. No curves to get wrong at small sizes, no stroke weights to rebalance per size, and it is trivially animatable later: the bars can settle in sequence on app launch, 90 ms apart.

The failure mode to design against

01 is one dot away from the generic filter icon that ships in every data table on the internet, including the one in Filtrsoft's own feed. If the third element were a third bar, the logo would be indistinguishable from a sort control, and worse, it would collide with the filter button in the app's own toolbar. That is why the bottom element is a rounded square, not a bar: it stops being part of the stack and starts being the thing the stack produced. If it ever gets "simplified" back into a bar, the mark is gone.

The second failure mode, and it applies to all nine. Nothing here has been drawn as outlined paths yet. The wordmark on this page is live Space Grotesk from Google Fonts, which means a shipped logo built this way would change shape if the font failed to load. The real deliverable is an SVG with the letters converted to paths and no font dependency at all.

One thing to weigh before I build it

01 says "filter". It does not say "value". Half of what Filtrsoft does is telling you what a thing is really worth, and a narrowing stack says nothing about money or about hidden defects. 07 The Scan is the only mark that gestures at the reading-the-listing half, and it is a worse icon in every other respect.

What would flip me: if the pitch you actually give people is "it catches the stuff you can't see" rather than "it cuts 4,000 listings to 28", then 07 is the right mark and I would redraw it to hold up at 16 px. Say which sentence you use when you describe the app to someone and I will take the mark from that.

Nothing in .brand The wordmark markup stays as it is unless you pick a different treatment. The mark is an added element, not a replacement.
The maskable icon's safe zone This is the one that bites. Android crops maskable icons to a circle at 80%, so the mark has to shrink inside that file specifically or the top bar of 01 loses its ends. make-icons.mjs needs a separate inset for that size.

Order I would build it

  • 1. The SVG, letters outlined, no font. Reversible, and it is the only artifact that matters. Everything else derives from it.
  • 2. Swap web/public/icon.svg and rerun make-icons. Four PNGs regenerate. Check the maskable one on a circle before anything else.
  • 3. Add the mark to .brand. One element, 22 px, in the existing 44 px row. This is the only app code that changes.
  • 4. Favicon and the landing page. Last, because it is the only one anyone who is not you will ever see.

What this page cannot show you

  • Real font rendering at your DPI. Space Grotesk here is Chrome on your monitor, not the phone screen where you actually use the app.
  • The icon on a real Android home screen, cropped to whatever mask your launcher uses, next to other apps' icons competing for attention. That test needs a real install.
  • How it ages. Every one of these looks fine today. The only one I would still be happy with in two years is the one that is three shapes.
