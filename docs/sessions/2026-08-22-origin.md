## How we got here

Yish opened with: he has heavy vibe-coding ability, entrepreneurial drive, wants to build an app that makes money, knows an app alone usually isn't enough (needs an ecosystem), and plans to build a social following partly for brand deals. Question was "what should the app do."

Committed **20 hours/week**.

### Rejected direction 1 — local service operator software
Detailing ops, AI receptionist for solo operators, before/after content engine. **Rejected by Yish:** doesn't want anything detailing-related or "operator" or cliche. Advice he'd received elsewhere was to research an unfamiliar industry and apply AI to it.

### Rejected direction 2 — sixteen unfamiliar B2B industries
Screened against: ACV $200–1000/mo, buyer = user = budget holder, old hated incumbent software, fragmented, findable buyers, low blast radius, not AI-obvious.

Candidates covered: fire and life safety inspections, Phase I environmental site assessments, small-carrier DOT compliance, aviation compliance, industrial distributor RFQ quoting, restoration contractors, P&C insurance policy checking, Davis-Bacon certified payroll, land surveying deed research, architectural spec writing, oil and gas land administration, equipment dealer warranty recovery, 811 locate tickets, small water system compliance, customs/HTS classification, food safety plans.

**Rejected by Yish** on two grounds: (a) wants nothing requiring government approval or dealing with the state, (b) too alien and dry to sustain passion for a year.

*Correction issued during this exchange:* his fear was aimed slightly wrong. The state doesn't review inspection software; the *inspector* is licensed and the software is just a tool. The filter that actually matters is **"don't sell to the government"** (procurement kills you), not "avoid regulated industries."

### Rejected direction 3 — RMV / DMV navigator
Yish's idea, from a real experience: lost a motorcycle plate, made **four trips** to the RMV, turned away each time for a different reason (not on the registration, missing a document, a pending vanity plate order in the system blocked a counter replacement).

**What was genuinely good and is worth keeping as a transferable pattern:** he wasn't blocked by the basics, he was blocked by **edge-case disqualifiers** that appear in no FAQ, aren't on the state site, and that a general AI hallucinates. A database of *why people actually get turned away*, harvested from real failed trips, is knowledge that exists nowhere public. **This pattern reappears below as the outcome feedback loop.**

**Killed by:** frequency. A consumer touches the RMV once every 2–4 years. No retention, no compounding word of mouth, every customer acquired fresh at the moment of pain, and consumers don't pay for information they can get free from ChatGPT. Monetization would have had to come from auto insurance lead gen (legally coupled to registration, highest-intent moment in that vertical) which requires a producer license and an adult. **Yish dropped it.**

### Rejected direction 4 — passion-adjacent industries
Business layer underneath things he loves: referee assigning, club/tournament operations, college athletic recruiting, private sports facilities, powersports repair, parts fitment, wholesale auction condition intelligence, independent educational consultants, custom apparel decorators, creator agency ops.

Not formally rejected. Superseded by the next idea.

## The idea we landed on

Yish's own, and it's the first one where his product goal and his social-following goal point the same direction.

He loves Facebook Marketplace, misses good deals, and used to **source gaming PC parts off Marketplace, build PCs, and resell them.** Original framing: an app that surfaces the very best Marketplace deals.

### The reframe that made it viable
Finding the listing was never the hard part. **Knowing what it's worth and what's wrong with it** was. So: build the valuation and risk engine, not the scraper. Marketplace becomes one input among many rather than the foundation.

### Scope decisions made this session
- **Category: all consumer tech.** Laptops, phones, GPUs, consoles, tablets. Not PC-only (too narrow an audience), not everything (no furniture, clothes, cars).
- **Form factor: standalone mobile app, not a browser extension.** Yish's call. Reasoning: Facebook's UI is already dense so an overlay adds clutter, an overlay is hostage to Meta's DOM forever, and a standalone app gives him UI freedom. The app deep-links out to the listing.
- **Target user: flippers first**, with a buyer mode as a second surface. Flippers use it daily (solves the frequency problem that killed the RMV idea) and treat it as a business expense.
- **Alerts are explicitly NOT in v1.** That's the commodity nine competitors fight over on price.

### The unifying thesis
**Used tech has binary, hidden value-destroyers that no price comp captures.** Not "worth 15% less" — worth nothing:

- iCloud Activation Lock (a $400 iPhone becomes a $40 parts unit)
- IMEI blacklisted (stolen or unpaid balance)
- Carrier locked vs unlocked
- EFI / firmware lock on a MacBook
- Battery health under 80%
- Mined-on GPU, banned console

Every competitor values the model. **None of them value the risk.** That generalizes Yish's PC-specific instinct across the whole category and is the core differentiator.

### Yish's stated #1 priority
**Reliability.** His words: the whole thing rests on whether strangers trust him to find the best listing, not miss one, and never hand them a scam. The engine has to be better than everyone else's. This is now the governing product principle and it drives the architecture (see `../ARCHITECTURE.md` once written).

## Research findings (full report in `../research-2026-08-22.md`)

**Category is crowded.** Nine-plus shipping competitors. flippr already ships the overlay + flip score + profit-after-fees at $12.99/mo. Underpriced AI already values items from photos at $12/mo. Swoopa charges $47–352/mo. pcprice.watch already does sum-of-parts PC valuation.

**But incumbents are bad.** Swoopa: 4.4 stars on 533+ ratings while reviews report filters that don't filter, 15-minute alert delays, and pricing called "a greedy joke." Price spread $5–352/mo for roughly the same thing means nobody has solved it. CarSnipe (cars-only) beating horizontal Swoopa in its niche proves verticalizing works.

**Key technical constraints found:**
- No Facebook Marketplace API, never has been
- Meta v. Bright Data: Meta **lost and dropped it**. ToS binds logged-in users only; logged-out collection of public data was not a violation
- eBay `findCompletedItems` EOL'd Feb 2025; Marketplace Insights closed to new applicants. Sold comps now cost money and only go back 90 days
- Vision AI ≈ $0.0085/listing on Haiku, $0.017 on Sonnet. Forces a funnel architecture
- iOS cannot run browser extensions at all (contributed to the mobile pivot)
- Manifest V2 fully dead Aug 31 2026 (moot now that we're mobile)
- eBay affiliate pays 1–2% on electronics, the worst category. Affiliate is not a revenue plan.

## Decisions log

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | All consumer tech, not PC-only | Bigger audience; the lock/blacklist thesis generalizes cleanly |
| 2 | Standalone mobile app, not extension | UI freedom, no DOM hostage, less clutter, iOS can't do extensions anyway |
| 3 | No alerts in v1 | Commoditized; nine competitors already fight there on price |
| 4 | Reliability is the governing principle | Yish's call. One scam link kills the brand. |
| 5 | Yish encodes the scam/risk rules himself | It's his actual domain knowledge and it's the moat |
| 6 | Store every comp ever pulled, from day one | eBay's 90-day window means an accumulated archive is uncopyable |
| 7 | Collection is bought (Apify/Bright Data), not built | Don't run the anti-bot arms race at 20 hrs/week |
| 8 | **Named Assyze.** Domain `assyze.com`. Folder `C:\Dev\assyze` | Respelling of *assize*: a formal judgment, a fixed standard of weight/measure/price, and the medieval market inspection that stopped merchants cheating buyers. See README for the full etymology. Chosen 2026-08-22 after five naming rounds (Loupe and Assay were taken or expensive; Greek and Hebrew options explored and dropped). Runners-up: Latent, Katch, Cupel. |
